Retirement Planning: Nominal Corpus vs Today’s Purchasing Power

The single greatest mistake in retirement planning is ignoring inflation. A 5,000,000 corpus 20 years in the future might sound substantial today, but at 6% annual inflation, it will have the equivalent purchasing power of approximately 1,560,000 in today’s money.

Our engine calculates your nominal future wealth first based on your expected investment return. Then, it separately applies your inflation assumption to determine what that corpus will actually buy in today’s goods and services.

Nominal Growth vs Real Purchasing Power

We never conflate returns with inflation. Nominal returns compound your bank account balance; inflation determines what goods and services cost.

Future Expense Multiplier

If you spend 40,000/month today, a 6% inflation rate over 20 years means you will need ~128,000/month for that exact same lifestyle.

Safe Withdrawal Strategy

Using the 4% safe withdrawal framework, your real retirement corpus provides sustainable monthly income without principal depletion.

Retirement Purchasing Power Discounting

Discounting future nominal corpus by cumulative inflation over the pre-retirement accumulation period: 

Real Value (Today's Purchasing Power) = Future Corpus / (1 + Inflation Rate)^Years

Example at 6% inflation over 16 years:
Real Value = 4,820,000 / (1.06)^16 = 1,897,000

Frequently Asked Questions

What inflation rate should I assume for retirement in India?▼

Consumer price inflation (CPI) in India has historically averaged around 5% to 7%. For planning retirement spanning several decades, 6% to 7% is a prudent baseline.

What is the 4% safe withdrawal rule?▼

The 4% rule suggests that a retiree can withdraw 4% of their initial retirement portfolio in the first year and adjust subsequent withdrawals for inflation, with high probability that the money will last 30+ years.
